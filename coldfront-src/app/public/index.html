Add a sensor

  1. Buy a Z-Wave temperature and humidity sensor (US 908 MHz). Zooz ZSE44 and Aeotec TriSensor 8 are common picks.
  2. Open Z-Wave JS UI (link in Settings), choose Manage Nodes → Add, and wake the sensor as its manual says.
  3. It appears here within a minute. Give it a name below.

      Future features

      Planned, not built yet. Each one needs a Z-Wave device on the same USB stick. Tell me which you want first. Buy US-frequency (908 MHz) Z-Wave devices, ideally 800 series, and check the listing mentions Z-Wave JS or Home Assistant.

      Water leak sensorPLANNED

      Alert on the Home screen and by ntfy when water shows up near the furnace, water heater or basement floor. Catches a failed condensate pump or leaking tank early.

      Buy: Z-Wave water leak sensor (battery).

      Door and window sensorsPLANNED

      Pause heating or cooling when a door has been open for 10 minutes or more. Insights would show how often doors get left open.

      Buy: Z-Wave door/window contact sensors (battery).

      Humidifier / dehumidifier plugPLANNED

      Run a plug-in dehumidifier or humidifier from a room's humidity reading and your humidity limits.

      Buy: Z-Wave smart plug rated for the appliance. Never put the furnace itself on a plug.

      Supply and return air probesPLANNED

      Measure the temperature difference across the furnace. A drop in it can point to a dirty filter or a weak heat exchanger. This would be the best diagnostic of the list.

      Buy: Z-Wave temperature sensor with an external probe (check the product supports a probe).

      Outdoor temperaturePLANNED

      Compare furnace runtime against real outdoor temperatures. Can use a Z-Wave outdoor sensor, or weather data from the internet at no cost.

      Buy: optional. Weather data needs no hardware.

      Furnace energy monitorPLANNED

      Real electricity use and cost for the blower and furnace, instead of the runtime-times-rate estimate.

      Buy: clamp-on energy meter at the electrical panel. Use an electrician to install it.

      Skip: smoke and CO alarmsNOT PLANNED

      Keep these as standalone alarms. Life-safety devices should not depend on this app.

      Connection

      Z-Wave setup checklist
      1. Plug the Zooz stick in with the extension cable, a few feet from the Umbrel.
      2. Open Z-Wave JS UI on port 8091 of your Umbrel (http://umbrel.local:8091).
      3. Settings → Z-Wave: pick the stick as the serial port, set RF region to USA, then Save.
      4. Settings → Home Assistant: turn on WS Server (port 3000). Keep Home Assistant mode off.
      5. Wake the T6 Pro and include it: Manage Nodes → Add. Wait for the interview to finish.
      6. Come back here, switch to Live.

      Filter reminder

      You set the dates by hand. Cold Front counts the days and reminds you.

      Notifications (ntfy)

      Sends push alerts to your phone through ntfy. Install the ntfy app, subscribe to a topic name only you know, and enter it here. Every alert below is off until you turn it on. Anyone who knows an ntfy.sh topic can read it, so pick a long random name or use your own ntfy server.

      Control

      Room-aware raises the heat setpoint when that room is colder than the schedule target, and lowers the cool setpoint when it is warmer. It re-checks every 10 minutes and never goes past your safety limits.

      Safety limits

      Nothing Cold Front sends can go outside these, whatever the schedule or a hold says.
